## Philosophical Foundations

The conceptual ground Fane is built on.

- **Fan Li / Tao Zhugong (陶朱公)** — the historical merchant-philosopher
  Fane is named after. The "Twelve Golden Rules of Business" attributed
  to him are compiled from various sources rather than a single
  authoritative text. Sima Qian's *Records of the Grand Historian*
  (*Shiji*) contains his biography and is the most authoritative
  primary source. Finding a good English treatment is itself a
  research task; flag any decent translation when encountered.

## Track 1 — Financial Markets

### Foundations

- **A Random Walk Down Wall Street** — Burton Malkiel
  *The efficient-markets perspective, fairly stated. Worth reading first
  so every other book is a response to this baseline.*
- **The Intelligent Investor** — Benjamin Graham
  *Value investing classic. Buffett's stated influence. Chapter 8 ("Mr. Market")
  and Chapter 20 ("Margin of Safety") are the durable core.*
- **Reminiscences of a Stock Operator** — Edwin Lefèvre
  *Thinly veiled biography of Jesse Livermore. Reads like a novel,
  teaches market psychology through narrative.*

### Going deeper

- **Stocks for the Long Run** — Jeremy Siegel
- **The Bond Book** — Annette Thau
- **Market Wizards** (series) — Jack Schwager
- **Trading in the Zone** — Mark Douglas

### Canadian context

- **The Wealthy Barber** — David Chilton
  *Introductory but specifically Canadian. Tax structures, RRSP/TFSA basics.*

## Track 2 — AI and LLMs as Financial Tools

- **Anthropic research papers and model cards** —
  https://www.anthropic.com/research
  *Primary source on capabilities and limitations of the models Fane uses.*
- **Thinking, Fast and Slow** — Daniel Kahneman
  *System 1 and System 2 thinking. Maps directly onto how LLMs behave
  and onto the flinch capture mechanism. Essential.*
- **Designing Machine Learning Systems** — Chip Huyen
  *For the infrastructure layer of ML/AI systems in production.*
- **The Alignment Problem** — Brian Christian
  *Where AI fails and why. Useful frame for thinking about model errors
  in financial contexts.*

### Papers and posts to track

- Anthropic's *Constitutional AI*, *Tool Use*, and *Computer Use* papers
- Research on LLM confidence calibration and hallucination detection
- Prompt engineering documentation at https://docs.claude.com

## Track 3 — Infrastructure and Plumbing

- **Designing Data-Intensive Applications** — Martin Kleppmann
  *The canonical text for understanding distributed systems, databases,
  and the trade-offs at every layer of modern infrastructure.*
- **How Linux Works** — Brian Ward
  *Systems thinking applied to Linux. Reinforces what is already known
  rather than starting from scratch.*
- **The Linux Programming Interface** — Michael Kerrisk
  *Reference text. Read sections as needed rather than cover to cover.*
- **The Tangled Web** — Michal Zalewski
  *Web security from first principles. Relevant for Fane's exposure surface.*

## Macro Foundations

### Inflation, monetary policy, debt cycles

- **Principles for Dealing with the Changing World Order** — Ray Dalio
  *The debt cycle framework, applied at civilizational scale.
  Pair with Dalio's free online "Economic Principles" video.*
- **The Lords of Easy Money** — Christopher Leonard
  *Recent history of the Fed. How modern monetary policy actually works.*
- **When Money Dies** — Adam Fergusson
  *Weimar hyperinflation. The visceral version of what inflation actually is.*

### Foreign exchange and reserve currencies

- **Currency Wars** — James Rickards
  *Polemical but informative on USD reserve dynamics.*
- **Lords of Finance** — Liaquat Ahamed
  *Central bankers in the interwar period. How currency regimes break.*

### Energy

- **The Prize** — Daniel Yergin
  *The history of oil. The physical layer of the global economy.*
- **The New Map** — Daniel Yergin
  *Sequel covering shale, renewables, and current energy geopolitics.*

### Banking and monetary plumbing

- **The Bankers' New Clothes** — Anat Admati and Martin Hellwig
  *How banks actually work, written for non-bankers.*
- **Concoda** (Substack) — ongoing coverage of repo markets and shadow banking
- **Money Mischief** — Milton Friedman
  *Historical case studies in monetary policy gone wrong.*

### Market microstructure and manipulation

- **Flash Boys** — Michael Lewis
  *HFT and dark pools, narrative-driven.*
- **Trading and Exchanges** — Larry Harris
  *The definitive academic text on market microstructure. Reference.*
- **Dark Pools** — Scott Patterson
  *History of electronic trading and its consequences.*

### Reflexivity

- **The Alchemy of Finance** — George Soros
  *Reflexivity as a theory of markets. Difficult prose, important ideas.*

### Demographics

- **The Great Demographic Reversal** — Charles Goodhart and Manoj Pradhan
  *How aging populations reshape inflation, interest rates, and inequality.*

## Market History

- **Manias, Panics, and Crashes** — Charles Kindleberger
  *The canonical history of speculative bubbles. Updated through multiple editions.*
- **Devil Take the Hindmost** — Edward Chancellor
  *History of financial speculation from antiquity forward.*
- **The Great Crash 1929** — John Kenneth Galbraith
  *Short, sharp, still the best account of 1929.*
- **When Genius Failed** — Roger Lowenstein
  *Long-Term Capital Management. The first modern liquidity crisis.*
- **The Big Short** — Michael Lewis
  *2008 told through the few who saw it coming.*
- **Too Big to Fail** — Andrew Ross Sorkin
  *2008 from the inside of the banks and Treasury.*
- **A Demon of Our Own Design** — Richard Bookstaber
  *Why modern financial systems are inherently fragile.*

## Behavioral and Philosophical

The lens for understanding why humans (and models) make the decisions they do.

- **Fooled by Randomness** — Nassim Taleb
  *The first and most accessible Taleb. Read before Black Swan.*
- **The Black Swan** — Nassim Taleb
- **Antifragile** — Nassim Taleb
- **Misbehaving** — Richard Thaler
  *Behavioral economics, written as memoir. Easier than Kahneman.*
- **Descartes' Error** — Antonio Damasio
  *The somatic marker hypothesis — the neuroscience of the flinch.
  Critical reading for understanding what Stage 3 is actually capturing.*

## Recurring Sources

### Podcasts

- **Odd Lots** (Bloomberg, Joe Weisenthal and Tracy Alloway)
  *The most consistent macro and microstructure podcast. Weekly listening.*
- **Macro Voices** — guest-driven macro discussions
- **Acquired** — deep historical dives on specific companies
- **Forward Guidance** (Blockworks) — Fed and rates focus
- **Invest Like the Best** (Patrick O'Shaughnessy)
- **The Memo by Howard Marks** — Oaktree founder, quarterly memos read aloud

### Newsletters and substacks

- **Money Stuff by Matt Levine** (Bloomberg) — daily, free
  *The single most valuable financial newsletter in existence.*
- **Net Interest by Marc Rubinstein** — weekly, banking and finance
- **The Diff by Byrne Hobart** — daily, finance + tech crossover
- **Doomberg** — energy and macro
- **Concoda** — repo and monetary plumbing
- **Things That Make You Go Hmmm** — Grant Williams
- **The Daily Shot** — daily macro charts

### Primary sources

- **SEC EDGAR** — https://www.sec.gov/edgar.shtml
  *All US company filings (10-K annual, 10-Q quarterly, 8-K events, 13F holdings).*
- **SEDAR+** — https://www.sedarplus.ca
  *Canadian equivalent of EDGAR.*
- **FRED** — https://fred.stlouisfed.org
  *St. Louis Fed economic data. Definitive source for US macro series.*
- **Bank of Canada** — https://www.bankofcanada.ca
  *Monetary Policy Reports, rate decisions, Governor speeches.*
- **Federal Reserve** — FOMC statements, minutes, projections
- **BIS** (Bank for International Settlements) — https://www.bis.org
  *Quarterly Review and Working Papers are gold for monetary plumbing.*
- **IMF** — World Economic Outlook, Global Financial Stability Report
- **Statistics Canada** — https://www.statcan.gc.ca
  *CPI, employment, GDP — Canadian macro source.*
